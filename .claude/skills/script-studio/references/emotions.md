# Emotions & Performance — the feeling catalog

How to make a character *feel* a precise emotion and show it perfectly on screen —
face, body, eyes, breath, voice. Descriptions are written **prompt-ready** for AI
video generation and for directing performers. Covers humans **and animals**.

## How emotion reads on screen (the craft)
- **Emotion comes from a want blocked or met.** A character feels because they're chasing
  something and hit an obstacle or a reward. Give the emotion a *cause* — don't just label it.
- **Show, don't tell.** The body leaks the feeling; we read it, no one announces it.
- **Micro vs macro.** A macro-expression is full and held; a **micro-expression** flashes
  (~½ second) when a character *hides* a feeling — the truth leaking through a mask. Use
  micros for subtext and lies.
- **Emotions transition.** The most powerful moments are the *turn* — grief cracking into
  rage, fear melting into relief. Play the shift, not a static state.
- **Layering (subtext).** People feel two things at once (a smile hiding pain). The gap is drama.

## The 7 basic emotions (Ekman) — universal facial tells

| Emotion | Eyes / brows | Mouth / face | Body & voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Happiness / Joy** | crow's-feet crinkle, eyes narrow ("Duchenne" — real smile reaches the eyes) | corners of lips up, cheeks raised | open, light, energized; warm rising voice |
| **Sadness** | inner brow corners up & together, gaze down, eyes may well | lip corners pulled down, chin quivers, lower lip out | slumped shoulders, slow, heavy; quiet, cracking, low voice |
| **Fear** | brows raised & drawn together, upper eyelids lifted, whites showing | lips stretched horizontally, mouth may open | tense, frozen or flinching, leaning back; breath sharp, shaky/high voice |
| **Anger** | brows down & together, hard glare, lower lid tense | lips pressed thin or bared teeth, jaw clenched | forward, chest out, fists; low growl or explosive volume |
| **Surprise** | brows high & curved, eyes wide | jaw drops, mouth open | jolt/recoil, still for a beat; sharp intake of breath |
| **Disgust** | lower lids raised, nose wrinkled | upper lip raised, tongue may show | pulling away, turning head; "ugh," recoil |
| **Contempt** | one brow may lift, cool gaze | **one-sided** lip corner tightens/raises (asymmetric) | chin up, looking down at; dismissive tone |

## Plutchik's 8 primaries × intensity (dial the strength)

Each primary runs from mild → strong. Naming the intensity is how you get *precision*:
- **Joy**: serenity → joy → ecstasy
- **Trust**: acceptance → trust → admiration
- **Fear**: apprehension → fear → terror
- **Surprise**: distraction → surprise → amazement
- **Sadness**: pensiveness → sadness → grief
- **Disgust**: boredom → disgust → loathing
- **Anger**: annoyance → anger → rage
- **Anticipation**: interest → anticipation → vigilance

**Blends (dyads)** — two primaries combine into a complex emotion:
joy+trust = **love** · joy+anticipation = **optimism** · trust+fear = **submission** ·
fear+surprise = **awe** · surprise+sadness = **disappointment** · sadness+disgust = **remorse** ·
disgust+anger = **contempt** · anger+anticipation = **aggressiveness**.

## Complex & social emotions (the rich palette)
Each below = feeling → the key physical tell to play it:
- **Love / tenderness** → soft eyes, relaxed face, leaning in, gentle touch, warm slow voice.
- **Longing / yearning** → distant gaze, slight reach, a held breath, wistful half-smile.
- **Pride** → chin & chest lifted, small smile, expansive posture.
- **Shame** → head & gaze down, shoulders curl in, face flushes, hiding/covering.
- **Guilt** → avoiding eye contact, fidgeting, tight mouth, restless hands.
- **Embarrassment** → blush, nervous laugh, touching face/neck, looking away.
- **Jealousy / envy** → narrowed watching eyes, tight jaw, a fixed stare at the rival/object.
- **Contempt / disdain** → asymmetric lip, eye-roll, chin up, slow dismissive blink.
- **Awe / wonder** → widened eyes, dropped jaw, stillness, a slow exhale, gaze up.
- **Hope** → lifted gaze, softening face, breath opening, a forward lean.
- **Despair / hopelessness** → collapsed posture, empty stare, slow or no movement, flat voice.
- **Relief** → exhale, shoulders drop, face releases, a shaky laugh or tears.
- **Gratitude** → soft eyes, small nod, hand to chest, warm voice.
- **Anxiety / worry** → darting eyes, fidgeting, shallow breath, lip-biting, restless.
- **Determination / resolve** → fixed focused gaze, set jaw, steady breath, grounded stance.
- **Confusion** → furrowed brow, head tilt, squint, mouth slightly open, pause.
- **Boredom** → droopy eyes, slack face, sighs, fidget, checking out.
- **Loneliness** → small in the frame, closed posture, distant look.
- **Nostalgia** → soft distant smile, gaze middle-distance, gentle warmth tinged with sadness.
- **Curiosity / interest** → leaning in, bright eyes, raised brows, quickened attention.
- **Suspicion / doubt** → one brow up, sidelong glance, pursed lips, guarded body.
- **Disappointment** → face falls, gaze drops, small sigh, deflated posture.
- **Panic** → wide eyes, rapid breath, jerky movement, high voice, loss of control.

## The physical vocabulary (build any expression)
- **Eyes**: direction (down=shame/sad, up=hope/awe, away=guilt/lie), openness (wide=fear/
  surprise, narrow=anger/joy), tears, blink rate (fast=stress), pupil.
- **Brows**: up (surprise/fear), down+together (anger/concentration), inner-up (sadness).
- **Mouth/jaw**: smile type (real=eyes crinkle, fake=mouth only), pressed lips (anger/holding
  back), quiver (about to cry), clenched jaw (tension).
- **Body**: open/expansive (confidence, joy) vs closed/small (fear, shame); forward (anger,
  interest) vs back/away (fear, disgust); still (shock, awe) vs restless (anxiety).
- **Hands**: fists (anger), wringing/fidget (anxiety), to face/mouth (shock, shame), to chest
  (love, gratitude), reaching (longing).
- **Breath**: sharp intake (fear/surprise), held (tension), slow exhale (relief/awe), shaky (crying).
- **Voice**: pitch (high=fear/excitement, low=anger/sadness), pace, volume, cracks, silence.

## Intensity, restraint & the reveal
- **Restraint reads as strength** — a single tear from a still face hits harder than sobbing.
- **Suppression + leak** — a character holding it together while a micro-expression betrays them
  is the most cinematic emotional beat.
- **Build to the break** — earn a big emotion; let pressure rise, then release at the turn.

## Animal emotions (how animals show feeling on camera)
Animals emote through **ears, tail, posture, eyes, fur/feathers, and vocalization**. General
mammalian tell: **piloerection** (hair/hackles up) = fear or aggression.

- **Dog** (27 facial actions): tail high/flagging = confident/aroused, wagging loose = happy,
  tucked = fear/submission; ears forward = alert/interest, pinned back = fear/appeasement;
  "play bow" (front down, rear up) = playful; lips lifted/teeth bared + stiff body = threat;
  soft eyes + relaxed open mouth ("smile") = content; whale-eye (whites showing) = stress.
- **Cat**: tail up = confident/friendly, puffed = alarm/aggression, low/tucked = fear, tip
  twitching = irritation; ears forward = curious, sideways = uneasy, flat "airplane" = anger/fear;
  slow blink = trust/affection; arched back + puffed = threatened; kneading/purr = content.
- **Horse** (17 facial actions): ears forward = interested, pinned back = anger/threat; high
  tail = alert/excited, clamped = fear/about to kick; wide nostrils + white eye = fear; relaxed
  lower lip/soft eye = calm.
- **Birds**: raised crest/feathers = alert or aggression, sleek = calm; wings out/open beak =
  threat; fluffed = content or unwell (context); rapid tail bob = stress.
- **Prey animals (rabbits, deer)**: freeze = fear, ears swivel = scanning, flight = panic,
  relaxed flop = safe/content.

Direct animal emotion the same way: give it a *cause* (a sound, a threat, a reunion) and show
the tell. For AI generation, name species + emotion + the specific body signal.

## Amplify emotion with the rest of the studio
- **Camera**: push-in on the face as feeling peaks; tight CU/ECU for intimacy; low angle for
  power/rage, high angle for vulnerability (see `camera-angles.md` / `camera-movement.md`).
- **Color/light**: warm for love/joy, cold/desaturated for grief/fear, drain color at despair,
  bloom it at relief (see `cinematography-color.md`).
- **Sound/pace**: silence and a held shot for grief; fast cuts and rising score for panic/joy.

## Prompt-ready notation
`EMOTION: <named + intensity> | CAUSE: <why> | FACE: <eyes·brows·mouth> | BODY: <posture·hands·breath> | VOICE: <pitch·pace> | MICRO: <leak, if hidden>`
e.g. `EMOTION: grief (held) | CAUSE: reads the letter | FACE: inner brows up, gaze down, single tear | BODY: still, shoulders drop, one hand to mouth | VOICE: silent | MICRO: jaw trembles once.`
Animal: `SPECIES: dog | EMOTION: fear | SIGNAL: ears pinned, tail tucked, whale-eye, low crouch.`
