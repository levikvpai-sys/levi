# The Director of Photography — Shots, Angles, Movement & Composition

Every shot is a choice about *how the viewer feels*. Size controls intimacy, angle
controls power, movement controls energy, composition controls the eye. Never
default to "medium shot, eye level" — pick with intent.

---

## Shot sizes (intimacy / information)

| Shot | Frames | Feeling / use |
|---|---|---|
| **Extreme Wide (EWS)** | subject tiny in vast space | scale, isolation, establishing the world |
| **Wide / Long (WS)** | full body + environment | context, geography, "where we are" |
| **Full Shot (FS)** | head-to-toe | body language, action, fashion |
| **Medium Wide (MWS)** | knees up | conversation with context |
| **Medium (MS)** | waist up | neutral, dialogue workhorse |
| **Medium Close-Up (MCU)** | chest up | the interview/talking-head standard |
| **Close-Up (CU)** | face fills frame | emotion, intimacy, reaction |
| **Extreme Close-Up (ECU)** | eyes/mouth/detail | intensity, a critical detail, tension |
| **Insert** | an object detail | plot-relevant object (phone, hand, clue) |

Rule of thumb: **the tighter the shot, the bigger the emotion.** Push in as stakes rise.

---

## Camera angles (power / psychology)

- **Eye level** — neutral, honest, relatable. The default for realism and connection.
- **Low angle** (looking up) — power, dominance, heroism, threat. Makes subject big.
- **High angle** (looking down) — weakness, vulnerability, smallness, being watched.
- **Dutch / canted** (tilted horizon) — unease, tension, disorientation, madness.
- **Overhead / bird's-eye / top-down** — god's-eye, pattern, isolation, choreography;
  great for reveals and satisfying "flat-lay" content.
- **Over-the-shoulder (OTS)** — puts us in a conversation, shows relationship/POV.
- **Point-of-view (POV)** — we *become* the character; immersive, first-person.
- **Worm's-eye** (extreme low) — awe, monumentality, overwhelming scale.
- **Profile / 90°** — confrontation, division, "two sides."

Power pairing: cut a **low angle** on the strong character against a **high angle**
on the weak one to encode who's winning without a word.

## Camera movement (energy / meaning)

- **Static / locked-off** — stability, composition, tension by stillness; lets the
  performance carry.
- **Pan / Tilt** — reveal horizontally/vertically from a fixed pivot.
- **Dolly / Truck** — camera physically moves in/out or side-to-side; smooth, motivated.
- **Push-in (dolly-in)** — creep toward a face → rising tension/realization. The most
  emotionally reliable move.
- **Pull-out** — reveal context or abandon a character (isolation).
- **Tracking / following** — travel with a moving subject; energy, urgency.
- **Crane / jib** — vertical sweep; grandeur, scope, the "big" moment.
- **Handheld** — raw, urgent, documentary, chaos, immediacy.
- **Steadicam / gimbal** — floating, dreamlike smoothness through space.
- **Whip pan** — fast blur; energetic transition, comedy, momentum (great short-form cut).
- **Zoom** — optical, not physical; snap-zoom = punch/emphasis (meme/comedy energy).
- **Dolly zoom (Vertigo)** — dolly one way + zoom the other → world warps; dread/realization.
- **Arc / orbit** — circle the subject; intensity, disorientation, "hero moment."

Movement = emotion in motion. Match the move to the beat (push-in on a confession,
handheld on panic, static on dread).

## Composition (guide the eye)

- **Rule of thirds** — place subjects/eyes on the third-lines; balanced, dynamic.
- **Center framing** — symmetry, control, formality (Kubrick/Wes Anderson), or unease.
- **Leading lines** — roads, rails, arms point the eye to the subject.
- **Symmetry & balance** — order, beauty; asymmetry creates tension/unease.
- **Depth / layering** — foreground + midground + background = a 3D, immersive frame.
- **Negative space** — emptiness around a subject = loneliness, scale, calm.
- **Frame within a frame** — doorways, windows, mirrors → focus, entrapment, voyeurism.
- **Headroom** — right amount above the head; too much = lost, too little = trapped.
- **Lead room / nose room** — space in the direction a subject looks/moves; its absence
  = tension.
- **Foreground obstruction** — shoot through objects → intimacy, secrecy, depth.
- **180° rule** — keep the camera on one side of the action line so screen direction
  stays consistent (break it deliberately for disorientation).

## Lens language (the invisible feeling)

- **Wide lens (14–35mm)** — expansive, exaggerated depth/distortion, immersive, energetic;
  close-up wides feel intense/comic. Great for POV and action.
- **Normal (~50mm)** — human-eye natural, honest.
- **Telephoto (85mm+)** — compresses depth, isolates subject, creamy background blur
  (bokeh); flattering for faces, voyeuristic for surveillance.
- **Shallow depth of field** — subject sharp, background melts → focus & "cinematic."
- **Deep focus** — everything sharp → information, realism, staging in depth.
- **Focal length changes feeling**: same framing, wide lens up close feels aggressive;
  long lens far away feels observed/distant.

---

## What's most *engaging* for short-form (the "what fascinates people" answer)

- **Motion in the first frame** — a moving/DTF (developing) shot out-hooks a static one.
- **Push-ins and snap-zooms** on key lines add subconscious energy.
- **Fast cut rhythm** (1.5–3s) with angle *changes* between cuts keeps the eye fresh —
  don't cut between two near-identical framings (jarring "jump cut" unless intentional).
- **Pattern-interrupt angles** — an unexpected overhead, a whip-pan, a Dutch tilt —
  reset attention mid-video (re-hook).
- **Faces close and centered** for talking-head retention; **B-roll wide/detailed**
  for context and satisfaction.
- **Satisfying reveals** via pull-out or overhead pay off curiosity.
- **Change *something* every few seconds** — size, angle, or movement — so no shot idles.

## How to notate a shot (shotlist format)

```
#  | SIZE | ANGLE     | MOVEMENT   | LENS  | DESCRIPTION / ACTION            | DURATION
01 | ECU  | eye level | push-in    | 85mm  | Eyes flick up on the reveal     | 1.5s
02 | WS   | low angle | static     | 24mm  | Hero framed against the skyline | 2s
03 | MCU  | Dutch     | whip-pan in| 35mm  | Turn to camera, deliver punch   | 1s
```

Always specify **size + angle + movement** at minimum; add lens/duration when it matters.
Tie each shot to its beat so the coverage serves the story, not the reel.
