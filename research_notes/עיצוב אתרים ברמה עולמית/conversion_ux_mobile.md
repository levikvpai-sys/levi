# Conversion-focused UX for a premium creative studio site (LEVIK Production): mobile, Israel/Hebrew norms, WhatsApp / form / call leads

Research method note: the evidence comes from 16 web searches (Nov 2026). Full-page fetches of unbounce.com, jpost.com, datareportal.com and melnik.org.il were blocked by this session's network allowlist. Figures attributed to those sites come from search-result summaries and the secondary pages that quote them, and are flagged where that matters. Little rigorous, peer-reviewed CRO research exists that is specific to creative studios. Most "studio" guidance is practitioner opinion, and these notes label it that way.

## 1. Homepage structure, CTA placement, form length and social proof

### Takeaway
No source has a studio-specific A/B test that sets a "correct" section order. The strongest evidence says to: (a) put the value proposition, proof and the primary CTA in the first one or two screens, because attention drops sharply below the fold; (b) put trust signals right next to the CTA; and (c) keep the lead form short (about 3–5 fields). Mobile is where most traffic lands, but it converts somewhat worse than desktop, so mobile friction is the main lever.

### Cited Findings
- NN/g eyetracking (2018): users spent **57% of viewing time above the fold** and **74% in the first two screenfuls**. The 2010 figure was about 80% above the fold. Users do scroll, but attention falls off steeply. — [NN/g via search summary](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/)
- NN/g homepage principles: the most important content goes above the fold, and the design should lead users down the page when there is more below. — [NN/g, Homepage Design: 5 Fundamental Principles](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/)
- NN/g (113 homepage guidelines): awards, recognition and favorable reviews from independent authorities raise credibility. Awards for the quality of the product or service build more trust than awards for site design. This matters for a studio tempted to show "Awwwards"-type badges. — [NN/g, 113 Design Guidelines for Homepage Usability](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/113-design-guidelines-homepage-usability/)
- NN/g trustworthiness research: a cleaning company that lacked testimonials and photos for one service line looked less committed to that service. People also trust testimonials on **external sites more than those on the company's own site**, which argues for linking to Google reviews and similar platforms. — [NN/g, Trustworthiness in Web Design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)
- NN/g: social proof reduces decision uncertainty by showing that other people, ideally familiar ones, like the product. The article is about 7 years old. — [NN/g, Social Proof in the User Experience](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/social-proof-ux/)
- CXL eyetracking: testimonials **with photos** produced significantly better recall than testimonials without photos. This is agency research, not peer-reviewed. — [CXL, Social proof](https://cxl.com/blog/is-social-proof-really-that-important/)
- Practitioner guidance (not research): place the strongest trust signals next to the primary CTA, for example a testimonial just above the form or a relevant client logo under the headline. A small piece of proof above the fold, such as a rating or a short quote, is recommended. Avoid stock photos in testimonials. Generic quotes that would fit a competitor add nothing. — [Seed-light](https://seed-light.com/blog/homepage-design/); [GradeMyPage](https://grademypage.com/blog/social-proof-that-works-testimonials-logos-case-studies); [SimplyBook.me](https://news.simplybook.me/above-the-fold-design-tips-how-to-make-a-great-first-impression-on-your-visitors/)
- Unbounce 2024 Conversion Benchmark Report: **464M+ visitors, 41,000+ landing pages, 57M conversions** (July 23, 2023 – July 23, 2024). The **median conversion rate across industries was 6.6%**. **83% of visits were on mobile**, yet **desktop converted 8% better**. In some industries the gap is larger: health and wellness converts 22% better on desktop while getting 7x more mobile traffic. — [Newswire press release](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/unbounce-s-2024-conversion-benchmark-report-proves-that-attention-spans-are-declining-and-so-are-conversion-rates-831617439.html); [Unbounce report page](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/) (could not fetch)
- Form length (secondary sources, **unverified and partly conflicting**): figures attributed to Unbounce data are about 13% conversion for 1 field, about 10% for 3, about 8% for 5, about 5% for 7 and about 4% for 9+. Another source gives 10.1% for 3 fields and 3.6% for 9 fields. The commonly quoted "3 fields ≈ 10%" figure actually comes from Omnisend via HubSpot and covers ecommerce sign-up forms. — [Angarum Media 2026 CRO report](https://angarummedia.com/research/2026-conversion-rate-optimization-report/); [Bigdelta](https://bigdelta.com/blog/form-conversion-rate-benchmarks); [Colorlib](https://colorlib.com/wp/landing-page-statistics/)
- Baymard (ecommerce checkout, a proxy for form friction): the average checkout had **11.3 form fields across 5.1 steps in 2024**, down from 12.7–12.8 in 2019. Baymard's position is that most flows need only **about 8 fields**, and it demonstrated a reduction from 16 fields to 8. — [Baymard keynote, 16 → 8 fields](https://baymard.com/blog/checkout-optimization-from-16-fields-to-8); [GroundedKit verification of the 2024 figure](https://groundedkit.com/claims/baymard-average-checkout-5-1-steps-11-3-form-fields-2024/)

### Inferences
- A practical order for LEVIK: **Hero** (looping showreel or vertical video, a one-line promise in Hebrew, primary WhatsApp CTA and secondary "watch work" CTA, plus a micro-proof line such as client logos or "X films delivered") → **Selected work, 8–12 items**, filterable or split into Brands and Private/Events → **Proof**: logos plus 2–3 testimonials with faces or short video → **Services/packages**, with "starting from" where possible → **Process in 3–4 steps**, including turnaround time → **FAQ**, which handles the AI objection, timelines, revisions and pricing → **Final CTA block** with a short form, WhatsApp and phone. That makes about 7–8 sections. Because of the 57%/74% attention data, the hero and first work row carry most of the weight.
- Use two audience paths. Brands and private bar/bat-mitzvah clients have different objections. A segmented CTA in the hero ("לעסקים" / "לאירועים") or in the WhatsApp prefill text lets each path stay short.
- Repeating the same primary CTA several times on a long page is consistent with the attention data (see section 6 on the sticky bar). Competing primary CTAs with different goals is a different matter, and no 2024–2026 study was found on that.
- Lead form: name, phone (Israeli users expect a phone callback), project type (a select field) and an optional free-text field. Four fields sits in the high-converting range in every dataset above.

### Gaps
- No studio- or agency-specific benchmark for homepage section order or number of sections was found.
- The Unbounce 2024 industry tables (agency, media/entertainment, events) and the form-field figures could not be verified at the primary source, because the fetch was blocked.
- No 2024–2026 HubSpot primary data on single vs. multiple CTAs was found.

## 2. WhatsApp as a lead channel in Israel (wa.me, floating button, GA4 tracking, pairing with a form)

### Takeaway
WhatsApp is effectively universal in Israel, at 99% usage in 2025, the highest of any platform. A wa.me link with a prefilled, segment-specific message should be the primary mobile CTA, tracked in GA4 as a key event and backed up by a short form and a phone link.

### Cited Findings
- Israel Internet Association (איגוד האינטרנט הישראלי) 2025 annual survey, published early 2026: **WhatsApp at 99% usage** (+1 point from 2024), the #1 platform, overtaking **YouTube at 97%**. **Telegram is at 71% and TikTok at 57%**. **ChatGPT is at 85%, above Instagram**. In the Arab sector YouTube leads (100%) ahead of WhatsApp (98%). Among teens aged 13–17, WhatsApp is at 98%. — [ISOC-IL data portal, 2025 social media usage](https://www.isoc.org.il/sts-data/social-media-usage-survey-2025); [Ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/digital/technews/article/hkeh19oalg); [Mako](https://www.mako.co.il/nexter-news/Article-e51c98a02202a91027.htm); [ICE](https://www.ice.co.il/research/news/article/1088428); [ISOC-IL teens 2025](https://www.isoc.org.il/sts-data/social-media-use-il-teens-2025)
- DataReportal: Israel's population was 9.45M in January 2025, with 6.82M active social media user identities. WhatsApp-specific DataReportal figures could not be confirmed because the fetch was blocked. — [DataReportal Digital 2025 Israel](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-israel); [Digital 2026 Israel](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-israel)
- Official WhatsApp format: `https://wa.me/<number>?text=<urlencoded text>`. The number is in full international format with no leading zero, brackets or dashes; for Israel this looks like `9725XXXXXXXX`. The prefilled text appears automatically in the chat's input field. — [WhatsApp FAQ, How to use click to chat](https://faq.whatsapp.com/5913398998672934)
- The official FAQ does not document adding the page title or URL to the prefill. Encoding it into `text=` is community practice. Unique prefill text per channel or page is the usual way to tell where a chat came from. — [freeCodeCamp forum](https://forum.freecodecamp.org/t/whatsapp-click-to-chat-link-to-include-page-title-and-url-in-pre-filled-message/339727); [u2l.ai wa.me guide](https://u2l.ai/blog/whatsapp-click-to-chat-link)
- GA4 tracking pattern (practitioner guides, not Google documentation): in GTM, add a "Click – Just Links" trigger with the condition **Click URL** matching `wa\.me|api\.whatsapp\.com`, then send a GA4 event such as `whatsapp_click` and mark it as a **Key event** (formerly "conversion"). A common mistake is using Page URL instead of Click URL, which means the trigger never fires. GA4 Enhanced Measurement does not reliably capture WhatsApp or `tel:` clicks. Some guides reserve `generate_lead` for submitted forms and use separate `whatsapp_click` and `click_to_call` events. Test with GTM Preview and GA4 DebugView. — [PaidMediaWorld](https://paidmediaworld.com/track-whatsapp-leads-ga4-google-tag-manager/); [Dondy](https://www.dondy.net/post/guide-tracking-whatsapp-widget-clicks-using-google-tag-manager-gtm); [Hitesh Jaganiya](https://www.hiteshjaganiya.com/blog/track-whatsapp-enquiries-ga4); [palvdm](https://palvdm.com/blog/ga4-conversion-setup/)
- Floating and sticky elements: practitioner guidance warns against sticky bars or buttons that cover content, form fields or the keyboard, or that stack on top of a chat widget and cookie banner. Check for overlaps before launch and follow WCAG 2.2 target size. — [Global Bridge Labs](https://globalbridgelabs.co.uk/blogs/sticky-cta-mobile); [Convertica](https://convertica.org/ecommerce-case-study-sticky-cta/)

### Inferences
- Use prefills per entry point, written in Hebrew and short. Examples: "היי LEVIK, ראיתי את [שם הפרויקט] ואשמח לשמוע על סרט לבר/בת מצווה" and "היי, אני מ[חברה] ומתעניינים בסרט פרסומת AI". Each works as both a lead qualifier and a source tag.
- Hebrew is RTL, so on mobile the floating WhatsApp button conventionally sits **bottom-left**, the "end" side in RTL, or is built into a full-width sticky bottom bar. Keep it clear of the cookie banner and of the accessibility-widget icon, which Israeli sites very commonly place in a corner. This is design inference; no source measured placement.
- A WhatsApp click is a *click*, not a confirmed lead. Report WhatsApp clicks, form submits (`generate_lead`) and `tel:` clicks as separate key events, and reconcile them against the conversations actually received.
- Pair it with a form for people who prefer not to start a chat, such as corporate brand managers, and for desktop users, where wa.me opens WhatsApp Web or Desktop.

### Gaps
- No Israeli data on WhatsApp vs. form vs. phone *conversion* share for service businesses was found.
- No Meta or WhatsApp Business documentation on floating-button placement was found.

## 3. Instagram-to-site traffic: link in bio, the first 3 seconds, vertical video

### Takeaway
Instagram visitors land inside Instagram's in-app browser on a phone. They have no saved logins, they are impatient, and they arrive expecting the same vertical visual language they just saw. The landing view must load fast (under about 2–3 s) and show the work and a WhatsApp CTA immediately. The evidence here is almost entirely from vendor blogs.

### Cited Findings
- Bio links open in Instagram's in-app browser, which does not share the phone's saved logins or passwords. That breaks login and payment flows. Fixes include detecting the in-app browser and offering "open in browser", or deep links to native apps. Some vendors claim a Linktree-style page doesn't help, because every link on it still opens inside Instagram. — [kode.link](https://blog.kode.link/social-media-growth/how-to-build-a-good-bio-for-instagram-and-optimize-your-link-strategy/); [mysocial.io](https://mysocial.io/blog/instagram-bio-link-smartlink/); [zori.bio](https://zori.bio/blog/in-app-browser); [instantdm](https://instantdm.com/blog/how-to-add-a-link-to-an-instagram-bio)
- Page-level analytics can break inside in-app browsers, so vendors advise tracking at link level, for example with UTMs on the bio link. Tap your own bio link on a phone to see what visitors get. — [zori.bio](https://zori.bio/blog/in-app-browser)
- Speed target for bio-link landings is about 2 s on mobile, and pages taking more than 3 s lose a significant share of clicks. Heavy images, unoptimized scripts and **autoplay video** are named as the usual causes. — [Koblio](https://koblio.com/blog/instagram-link-in-bio-7-settings-to-maximize-your-clicks); [Mobilocard](https://www.mobilocard.com/post/how-to-add-link-to-instagram-profile)
- Replace a generic "link in bio" with a specific CTA, and put the primary CTA at the top of the bio. — [FastLinkIt](https://blog.flnk.it/link-in-bio-instagram-the-ultimate-2026-guide-to-boosting-conversions/); [Mobilocard](https://www.mobilocard.com/post/how-to-add-link-to-instagram-profile)
- Vendor statistics that circulate widely (64% abandonment, 3–5x CTR, bounce above 50%) **have no primary source**. Do not cite them as fact. — [search summary of the above vendor blogs](https://zori.bio/blog/in-app-browser)

### Inferences
- Point the Instagram bio link to a UTM-tagged URL. Either use the homepage with its hero tuned for mobile, or a dedicated `/ig` landing with three things: a 9:16 reel of the best work, two tiles ("סרט לאירוע" / "סרט לעסק"), and a WhatsApp button. The first 3 seconds should answer "is this the studio from the reel I just saw, and how do I talk to them?"
- Vertical video on mobile: show 9:16 work natively in portrait rather than letterboxed inside 16:9 frames. Use a lightweight poster image plus a muted, short autoplay loop with lazy loading, and only load full-quality video when the user taps. This avoids the autoplay-speed penalty noted above. This is design inference; no study on 9:16 portfolio grids was found.
- Because WhatsApp is a native app, a wa.me tap from the in-app browser hands off to the WhatsApp app. That makes it a lower-friction CTA from Instagram than a multi-field form. This is inference and is untested here.

### Gaps
- No Meta or Instagram primary data on in-app browser bounce or conversion was found.
- No research on 9:16 vs. 16:9 portfolio presentation and conversion was found.

## 4. Pricing transparency for creative services

### Takeaway
No rigorous study shows that publishing prices increases leads for creative or custom services. The evidence is anecdotal or comes from vendors. The consistent practitioner recommendation for custom work is "starting from" prices or ranges, or packages, which act mainly as a qualification filter.

### Cited Findings
- WebFX reportedly increased qualified lead volume by **67% within six months** of publishing packages and price ranges. This is a single vendor anecdote, not independently verified. — [Tom Wardman](https://tomwardman.com/blog/publish-pricing-transparency-online)
- Claims such as "transparent pricing pages ≈ 2x qualified leads" and a "Harvard study: +44% sales" circulate **without citations**. — [Blessed Arc Media](https://blessedarcmedia.com/blog/home-service-website-pricing-transparency); [Kitchen Remodeling SEO](https://kitchenremodelingseo.com/should-i-put-my-prices-on-my-website/)
- One skeptical analysis searched for academic evidence on posted prices and lead quality for service sites and found none. It notes that a HubSpot-cited statistic traces back to a single-vendor survey. — [Zealous Growth](https://zealousgrowth.com/resources/pricing-on-your-website-when-to-show-numbers-and-when-to-hide-them/)
- For custom or project-based work, a "starting at" price or range, or a request-a-quote flow, is recommended over fixed prices. "Starting at" gives a floor without locking the studio in. — [Studios-web](https://studios-web.com/posts/pricing-page-strategies-small-business.html); [Zealous Growth](https://zealousgrowth.com/resources/pricing-on-your-website-when-to-show-numbers-and-when-to-hide-them/); [Customer Impact (B2B)](https://www.customerimpact.be/en/blog/showing-pricing-b2b-website/)
- Main mechanism: price transparency works as a **qualification filter**, so sales time goes to promising leads. — [Customer Impact](https://www.customerimpact.be/en/blog/showing-pricing-b2b-website/); [Dave Schoenbeck](https://daveschoenbeck.com/the-pros-and-cons-of-displaying-pricing-on-websites/)

### Inferences
- For LEVIK, the **private/events segment** (bar/bat mitzvah films) is a consumer purchase where people compare prices, so "starting from ₪X" packages are likely to reduce anxiety and filter out mismatched budgets. For **brands**, "projects from ₪X" or "typical range" plus a quote flow fits better. Test this rather than assume it: for example, run 4–6 weeks with and without prices and compare WhatsApp leads that turn into calls.
- Showing what is included (length, revisions, delivery time) may matter as much as the number itself. No study confirms this.

### Gaps
- No A/B test or Israeli data on pricing display for video or event-film services was found.

## 5. Trust builders: logos, testimonials, numbers, "as seen in", process, guarantees, response time

### Takeaway
The best-supported trust builders are specific testimonials with real faces (or video), independent third-party reviews, recognisable client logos placed near the CTA, and awards for work quality. Process explanations and response-time promises are logical but no study measured them.

### Cited Findings
- Testimonials with photos produce significantly better recall than testimonials without photos (CXL eyetracking). Faces increase trust. — [CXL](https://cxl.com/blog/is-social-proof-really-that-important/)
- Users trust testimonials hosted on external sites more than those on the company's own site. A missing service-specific testimonial makes the company look less committed to that service. — [NN/g, Trustworthiness in Web Design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)
- Third-party awards and reviews boost credibility, and quality-of-service awards beat design awards. — [NN/g, 113 guidelines](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/113-design-guidelines-homepage-usability/)
- Specificity: a testimonial that would still work with a competitor's name swapped in does nothing. Stock photos in testimonials are a common mistake. — [GradeMyPage](https://grademypage.com/blog/social-proof-that-works-testimonials-logos-case-studies); [Yotpo](https://www.yotpo.com/blog/social-proof-marketing-examples/)
- Place proof next to the CTA: a testimonial above the form and a client logo under the headline. This is practitioner guidance. — [Seed-light](https://seed-light.com/blog/homepage-design/)

### Inferences
- For an **AI** studio, the key objection is "will it look fake?" The best trust builders are therefore before/after or "how it was made" clips, and short **video testimonials of parents at the event**. Emotional event films sell on seeing other families react.
- Link to Google Business reviews, which external-trust research favours, and show the star count and review count near the WhatsApp CTA.
- Response-time promises such as "מענה בוואטסאפ תוך שעה בשעות הפעילות" and a 3–4 step process with timeline are reasonable anxiety reducers. They are untested here, and the promise has to be kept.
- Use numbers only when they are real and specific, such as the number of films delivered or brands served.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 study quantifies the conversion effect of video testimonials, guarantees or response-time promises.
- No data was found on whether "made with AI" disclosure helps or hurts trust for commercial video buyers.

## 6. Mobile-first patterns: thumb zone, tap targets, sticky bottom bar, speed

### Takeaway
Meet WCAG 2.2 AA (24×24 CSS px minimum) but design to 44–48 px, which satisfies WCAG AAA and the iOS and Android guidelines. A sticky bottom CTA bar can lift mobile conversions, though only single vendor tests support this. Speed matters: bounce probability rises 32% as mobile load time goes from 1 s to 3 s.

### Cited Findings
- WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum), Level AA: pointer targets must be **at least 24×24 CSS px**, or have 24 px spacing from neighbouring targets, with exceptions. 24 px is a floor, not a recommendation. — [TabNav, SC 2.5.8](https://tabnav.com/academy/wcag/success-criterion-2.5.8); [AllAccessible](https://allaccessible.org/blog/wcag-258-target-size-minimum-implementation-guide); [DisabilityWorld](https://www.disabilityworld.org/toolkit/standards/wcag/2-5-8-target-size-minimum/)
- SC 2.5.5 Target Size (Enhanced), Level AAA: **44×44 CSS px**. Apple recommends **44×44 pt**, and Google/Android recommends **48×48 dp**. — [DisabilityWorld 2.5.5](https://www.disabilityworld.org/toolkit/standards/wcag/2-5-5-target-size-enhanced/); [Shopify dev docs](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/best-practices/performance/increase-tap-target-size); [govt.nz guidance](https://govtnz.github.io/web-a11y-guidance/ka/accessible-ux-best-practices/mobile-apps/mobile-app-a11y-by-feature-type/touch-and-pointer-target-sizing.html)
- Speed (Google/SOASTA 2017 models): bounce probability **rises 32% (relative) as page load goes from 1 s to 3 s**, 90% from 1 s to 5 s, and 123% from 1 s to 10 s. **53% of mobile visitors leave if a page takes longer than 3 s.** The data is old (2017) but still the most cited. — [Think with Google](https://thinkwithgoogle.com/data/page-load-time-statistics); [Deloitte, Milliseconds Make Millions](https://www2.deloitte.com/content/dam/Deloitte/ie/Documents/Consulting/Milliseconds_Make_Millions_report.pdf)
- Sticky bottom CTA bar: one vendor case study reported **+252.9% mobile order completion** over a 20-day test (98% confidence), with no significant desktop difference. This is ecommerce, a single test from about 2023–2024, published by the vendor. — [Convertica](https://convertica.org/ecommerce-case-study-sticky-cta/); see also [GoodUI pattern #41](https://goodui.org/patterns/41)
- Sticky bars must not cover content or the keyboard, trap focus, or clash with chat widgets and cookie banners. — [Global Bridge Labs](https://globalbridgelabs.co.uk/blogs/sticky-cta-mobile)

### Inferences
- A mobile sticky bottom bar fits LEVIK well: two or three equal-width buttons ("וואטסאפ" as primary, "התקשרו" via `tel:`, and optionally "הצעת מחיר", which scrolls to the form), each at least 48 px tall. Show it after the hero scrolls out of view, and hide it while the form is focused so the keyboard isn't covered.
- A premium look usually means heavy video. Use poster images, lazy-load everything below the hero, keep the hero loop short and compressed (or use a still on slow connections), and test on a mid-range Android inside the Instagram in-app browser.

### Gaps
- Steven Hoober's thumb-zone and one-handed-use findings could not be verified in the searches, so no numbers are given.
- No 2024–2026 Baymard or NN/g primary research on sticky CTAs for service sites was found.

## 7. Israeli legal requirements: accessibility (IS 5568), privacy (Amendment 13), cookies

### Takeaway
Business sites offering a service to the public must be accessible under Regulation 35 of the Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities (Service Accessibility) Regulations 2013, per **IS 5568 at level AA**, and must publish an accessibility statement. An exemption exists for a licensed "osek patur" (עוסק פטור) or a business with average annual turnover of **₪100,000 or less**. Sources conflict on whether IS 5568 maps to WCAG 2.0 or 2.1. Building to **WCAG 2.1 AA, with 2.2 target sizes**, satisfies both readings. Privacy Amendment 13 took effect **14 August 2025**. Israel has no cookie statute, but the Privacy Protection Authority's opinion of **25 February 2026** treats implied consent as generally insufficient. In practice that means a granular, European-style cookie banner and a clear privacy notice at the form.

### Cited Findings
- **Accessibility obligation:** public-facing websites and apps must be accessible. An accessible site is one adjusted to **IS 5568 Part 1**. The legal basis is Regulation 35ו of the Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities (Accessibility Adjustments for Service) Regulations. — [Kol Zchut (Hebrew)](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%94%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%A9%D7%AA_%D7%90%D7%AA%D7%A8%D7%99_%D7%90%D7%99%D7%A0%D7%98%D7%A8%D7%A0%D7%98_%D7%95%D7%90%D7%A4%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%A7%D7%A6%D7%99%D7%95%D7%AA_%D7%9C%D7%90%D7%A0%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%9D_%D7%A2%D7%9D_%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%92%D7%91%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%AA); [Kol Zchut, what is an accessible site](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/Project:נגישות); [law.co.il, 2013 regulations](https://law.co.il/en/news/2013/11/13/New-website-accessibility-regulations-enacted)
- **Level AA** is the required conformance level. — [TabNav](https://tabnav.com/he/info-center/accessibility-standard-5568-israel-law); [BOIA](https://www.boia.org/blog/israels-digital-accessibility-laws-an-overview)
- **WCAG version is contested.** Several sources say IS 5568 is based on **WCAG 2.0 AA**. Others say it follows **WCAG 2.1**. One claims an IS 5568:2020 edition aligned with WCAG 2.1 and a Part 1 update in September 2023, which could not be verified from an official source. — [BOIA](https://www.boia.org/blog/israels-digital-accessibility-laws-an-overview); [Deque](https://www.deque.com/mena-digital-accessibility-laws/israel/); [Clym](https://www.clym.io/regulations/israeli-standard-5568-is-5568); [accessiBe](https://accessibe.com/compliance/is-5568); [sgo.co.il 2026 guide](https://sgo.co.il/en/blog/website-accessibility-israel-2026/)
- **Accessibility statement:** Regulation 35ה requires a statement in a prominent place on the site, following IS 5568 guidance. — [ecom-isr guide](https://www.ecom-isr.com/3989/accessibility/website-accessibility-israel-guide/); [i24NEWS statement as an example](https://www.i24news.co.il/accessibility-declaration)
- **Exemptions:** Regulation 35ו(ז) exempts an osek patur or a business with average annual turnover of ₪100,000 or less, based on a three-year average. Regulation 35ו(ט) exempts businesses with turnover of ₪1M or less **for sites launched before the regulations took effect**, provided contact details are published; some sources say this was time-limited. One generator site claims "no small-business exemption since 2021", which conflicts with the others and is unverified. — [i24NEWS (quotes the regulation text)](https://www.i24news.co.il/accessibility-declaration); [A2Z](https://a-2-z.co.il/accessibility-sites/accessibility-law-and-revision/); [web-a.co.il](https://www.web-a.co.il/blog/%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A7-%D7%94%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%91%D7%90%D7%99%D7%A0%D7%98%D7%A8%D7%A0%D7%98/); [Jonny Studio generator (conflicting claim)](https://jonnystudio.co.il/tools/accessibility-statement/)
- **Enforcement risk:** statutory damages of up to **₪50,000 without proof of damage** are cited by a secondary English-language overview, and a law firm describes a "dramatic increase in accessibility class actions". The legal provision was not verified. — [DisabilityWorld Israel overview](https://www.disabilityworld.org/regulations/il/); [Bar Law](https://barlaw.co.il/?p=22706)
- **Privacy, Amendment 13:** effective **14 August 2025**. The definition of personal data now includes "online identifier" and "location data", which is how cookies, pixels and analytics IDs fall under the law. There is no cookie-specific statute. — [Kukie.io](https://kukie.io/blog/cookie-consent-israel-privacy-law); [Schwebel Law](https://schwebel-law.com/en/writing/privacy-policy-cookies-israel); [Safetica](https://safetica.com/resources/guides/israel-s-amendment-13-what-the-new-data-protection-law-means-for-your-business); [Legal500 Annual Privacy Review 2025](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/global/privacy/annual-privacy-review-2025-%E2%80%93-outlook-for-2026)
- **Consent:** the Privacy Protection Authority opinion of **25 Feb 2026** says consent must be informed and freely given, and silence or lack of objection is not consent. Implied consent on websites is generally insufficient. A European-style mechanism that separates essential, analytics and advertising cookies will usually also meet the Israeli opinion. Israeli case law remains limited, and regulators are expected to look to European guidance. — [David Melnik](https://www.melnik.org.il/en/privacy-notice-consent-israel-en/) (summary only, fetch blocked); [WebProm Design 2026](https://www.web-design.co.il/2026/04/gdpr-cookies-and-consent-what-israeli-website-owners-need-to-know-in-2026/); [Connect.co.il, marketing measurement](https://connect.co.il/en/blog/marketing-privacy-measurement/); [ConsentX](https://www.consentx.io/country/israel)
- One vendor (FlexyConsent) describes a "2026 guidance on online tracking" that requires a first-layer reject option. Other sources don't mention it, so it is **unverified**. — [FlexyConsent](https://flexyconsent.com/blog/israel-privacy-protection-law-cookie-consent-guide/)

### Inferences
- LEVIK's checklist:
  - Build to WCAG 2.1 AA with 24 px or larger targets. Add captions and subtitles to portfolio videos, a pause control for autoplay and background video, keyboard focus states, sufficient contrast over video heroes, and correct `lang="he"` / `dir="rtl"`.
  - Publish a Hebrew accessibility statement (הצהרת נגישות) linked in the footer, with a named accessibility contact.
  - Do not rely on an overlay widget alone. That is inference; overlays are common in Israel but don't make a site conform.
- Forms: place a short privacy notice at the point of collection covering purpose, who receives the data and that providing it is voluntary. Add a separate **unticked** checkbox for marketing consent. Israel's anti-spam law (Communications Law §30A) also governs marketing messages, but it was not researched here.
- Cookies: load GA4 and the Meta and TikTok pixels only after granular consent, for example via Google Consent Mode v2. Make sure the banner never overlaps the sticky WhatsApp bar on mobile.
