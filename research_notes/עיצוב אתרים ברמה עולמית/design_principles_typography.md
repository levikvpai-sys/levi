## 2. Typography: scales, weights, pairings, and the best Hebrew fonts for a premium cinematic site

### Takeaway
Premium dark sites use **one expressive display face at very large sizes and one quiet, highly legible text face**. For Hebrew, the best options split into free bilingual Google families (Heebo, Rubik, Assistant, Secular One, Karantina, Frank Ruhl Libre, Noto Sans/Serif Hebrew, IBM Plex Sans Hebrew) and commercial Israeli foundries (AlefAlefAlef's **Ploni/Almoni**, Fontbit "Fb" fonts, Masterfont, Fontef). Pairing works when the Hebrew and Latin share a design origin, or at least weight and x-height.

### Cited Findings
**Google Fonts Hebrew catalog (free, OFL, self-hostable)**
- One audit found **62 of 1,959 Google families include Hebrew**, all open source. — [baba: Hebrew fonts (56 compared)](https://www.itsbaba.com/hebrew-fonts)
- **Heebo**: Hebrew drawn by **Oded Ezer**, extending Christian Robertson's **Roboto** Latin; mastered by Meir Sadan. It pairs natively with Roboto. — [A-G-I: Heebo](https://a-g-i.org/design/heebo); [baba guide](https://www.itsbaba.com/hebrew-fonts/guide/for-websites)
- **Rubik**: variable family; Hebrew version credited to **Meir Sadan**. — [Google Fonts: Rubik about](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Rubik/about); [Serbyte: Rubik (variable, OFL)](https://www.serbyte.net/fonts/rubik)
- **Assistant**: a Hebrew + Latin family with a "contemporary sans serif Hebrew design". — [Google Fonts: Assistant](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Assistant)
- **Secular One**: Michal Sahar, 2016. Humanist sans with Hebrew, Latin and Latin-extended, **single weight** (display use only). — [RightFont: Secular One](https://rightfontapp.com/family/secular+one); [Figma fonts: Secular One](https://www.figma.com/fonts/secular-one/)
- **Karantina**: Rony Koch, 2021. A free Latin/Hebrew Google font with a condensed display character. Sources conflict on the designer credit. — [Luc Devroye type page](https://luc.devroye.org/fonts-25364.html)
- **Frank Ruhl Libre**: a serif linked to the **Fontef** foundry (Yanek Iontef) via its GitHub repo, with Latin designed for Hebrew. — [Google Fonts: Frank Ruhl Libre about](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Frank+Ruhl+Libre/about); [Adobe Fonts: Fontef](https://fonts.adobe.com/foundries/fontef)
- **Bellefair**: serif that "pairs well with Latin classical typography". **Noto Sans/Serif/Rashi Hebrew** cover full nikud on the web. — [baba guide](https://www.itsbaba.com/hebrew-fonts/guide/for-websites)
- **Alef** (HaGilda + Mushon Zer-Aviv, 2012): free, OFL, Hebrew + Latin webfont. — [Font Squirrel: Alef](https://www.fontsquirrel.com/fonts/alef); [Font Squirrel: Hagilda foundry](https://www.fontsquirrel.com/fonts/list/foundry/hagilda)
- A curated open-source list exists. — [GitHub: Awesome-Hebrew-Fonts](https://github.com/danielrosehill/Awesome-Hebrew-Fonts); [Luc Devroye: Hebrew font links](https://luc.devroye.org/hebrew.html)

**Commercial Israeli foundries**
- **AlefAlefAlef (AAA)**: **Ploni** by Avraham Cornfeld is a "precise, geometric multilingual typeface" with 8 styles covering 230 Latin, Hebrew and Cyrillic languages. It was launched as a "brother" to **Almoni**; a cursive Ploni-Yad also exists. MyFonts lists individual styles from **$450** and the full family at **$4,500**. — [AAA: Ploni](https://alefalefalef.co.il/en/font/ploni/); [AAA: Almoni](https://alefalefalef.co.il/en/font/almoni/); [MyFonts: Ploni](https://www.myfonts.com/collections/ploni-font-alefalefalef); [AAA Hebrew fonts catalog](https://alefalefalef.co.il/en/%D7%A4%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%98%D7%99%D7%9D)
- The AAA EULA includes a separate **Webfont license** for embedding as live text. One snippet says no more than **4 fonts can be combined within a single project**. Read the full terms. — [AAA EULA](https://alefalefalef.co.il/en/more/license/); [AAA purchase info](https://alefalefalef.co.il/en/?p=27)
- **Fontbit**: founded **1999** by Nadav Ezra and Haim Caduri. It describes itself as Israel's largest font producer, its fonts carry the "Fb" prefix (e.g., Fb Alfi, Fb Kalisher), and it did a project with Neville Brody for a large cellular company. — [MyFonts: Fontbit](https://myfonts.com/foundry/fontbit); [Luc Devroye: FontBit](https://luc.devroye.org/fonts-45197.html)
- **Masterfont**: an Israeli foundry on MyFonts (e.g., Eilat, Shavit, Megubeshet MF). — [MyFonts: Masterfont](https://www.myfonts.com/foundry/Masterfont/)
- **Typotheque** spent **6 years** building an extensive Hebrew program, an international premium option. — [AIGA Eye on Design: Typotheque Hebrew](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/6-years-of-research-an-unfamiliar-alphabet-how-typotheque-developed-their-extensive-new-hebrew-program/)

**Pairing and usage rules**
- Match Hebrew and Latin companions so **weight and x-height agree** (e.g., Heebo with Roboto). A Latin-only file silently falls back to the browser default for Hebrew, so confirm the file actually contains Hebrew glyphs. — [baba guide](https://www.itsbaba.com/hebrew-fonts/guide/for-websites)
- Keep display faces for headlines only: they "fail in forms, tables and body articles". — [baba guide](https://www.itsbaba.com/hebrew-fonts/guide/for-websites)
- System fonts (David, Miriam, Arial Hebrew) **are not licensed as webfonts**. — [baba guide](https://www.itsbaba.com/hebrew-fonts/guide/for-websites)
- Hebrew structure: letters **hang from the top line** rather than sitting on a baseline. The "mem height" is roughly the equivalent of Latin x-height. Hebrew has **no capitals**. — [Meir Sadan: Intro to Hebrew type](https://medium.com/@meirsadan/an-introduction-to-hebrew-type-98933e2fcb17); [Ariela Housman: Why Hebrew and English look terrible together](https://ariela-housman-x1ul.squarespace.com/blog/2018/2/5/why-hebrew-and-english-on-the-same-page-always-looks-terrible)
- In Dalton Maag's Ubuntu Hebrew trials, Hebrew height was set at **~90% of Latin cap height**. Full cap height looked too big; smaller looked weak next to the Latin. — [Canonical: Initial Hebrew trials](https://canonical.com/blog/intial-hebrew-trials?format=md)
- Equal line-heights for Hebrew and Latin are "usually an incorrect assumption": the baselines align but the line boxes start and end at different points. — [Ariela Housman](https://ariela-housman-x1ul.squarespace.com/blog/2018/2/5/why-hebrew-and-english-on-the-same-page-always-looks-terrible)
- 2026 typography trend: variable fonts enable kinetic and responsive type. — [Fontfabric 2026 trends](https://www.fontfabric.com/blog/10-design-trends-shaping-the-visual-typographic-landscape-in-2026/); [InspoAI: Typography trends 2026](https://www.inspoai.io/blog/typography-trends-2026)

### Inferences (practitioner recommendations; not sourced numbers)
- **Recommended LEVIK pairings:**
  1. *Premium/commercial:* **Almoni** (display, heavy weights, huge sizes) + **Ploni** (UI/body). Same foundry and mixed-script design, so the Latin "LEVIK", "AI" and client names match natively.
  2. *Free, strong:* **Karantina** or **Secular One** for short display lines + **Heebo** or **Assistant** for body, with Inter/Roboto-class Latin. Heebo with Roboto is the safest technical match.
  3. *Cinematic/editorial accent:* **Frank Ruhl Libre** (serif) for pull quotes or film titles against a sans body. A serif accent signals "film, editorial" rather than "tech/SaaS".
  4. Variable **Rubik** if kinetic or variable-weight hero type is planned (check the Hebrew axis range).
- **Type scale (typical premium dark-site practice):** hero display about 8–14vw (clamp() based), H2 about 48–72px, body 17–20px (Hebrew reads small, so go 1–2px larger than the Latin equivalent), small/meta text 13–14px. Two or three weights at most (e.g., Black/Bold display, Regular body, Medium for UI labels).
- Avoid the Inter + purple-gradient "AI fingerprint" (see section 3). A Hebrew-first type choice is itself a differentiator.
- Subset the webfonts (Hebrew + Basic Latin) and use `font-display: swap` for performance on a video-heavy site.

## 3. Color and contrast on dark UIs: a vivid gradient without the generic "AI purple gradient"

### Takeaway
Use a **near-black, slightly tinted base** (not #000), **off-white text**, and treat the teal→purple gradient as a **rare, meaningful signal** in one or two places, not as wallpaper. The "indigo/purple gradient + Inter + three rounded cards" bundle is a widely recognized tell of AI-generated sites, so LEVIK's gradient needs a specific origin and a specific job.

### Cited Findings
- Avoid pure **#000000**. It produces harsh contrast and **halation** (bright text appears to smear) and leaves no room to show elevation. Material Design recommends **#121212** as a dark surface. — [UXPlanet: Dark mode best practices](https://uxplanet.org/dark-mode-ui-design-best-practices-8d3a00a83924); [Atmos: Dark mode UI best practices](https://atmos.style/blog/dark-mode-ui-best-practices); [Netguru: Dark mode tips 2025](https://www.netguru.com/blog/tips-dark-mode-ui)
- Avoid pure #FFFFFF text and use off-white such as **#E0E0E0 / #DADADA**. — [Halo Lab: Dark UI tips](https://www.halo-lab.com/blog/dark-ui-design-11-tips-for-dark-mode-design)
- **Desaturate accents** on dark backgrounds: brand colors that look right on white "glow or vibrate" on dark. Material favors desaturated colors in dark themes. — [UXPlanet](https://uxplanet.org/dark-ui-design-11-tips-for-dark-mode-design-f075b7e01749); [Uxcel: 12 principles of dark mode](https://uxcel.com/blog/12-principles-of-dark-mode-design-627)
- Check contrast **against the actual elevated surface color**, not pure black. — [Atmos](https://atmos.style/blog/dark-mode-ui-best-practices)
- Use brand colors only for the most prominent branded elements (logo, primary button) to protect hierarchy. — [Wix Studio: Dark mode design](https://www.wix.com/studio/blog/dark-mode-design)
- One academic study found the dark background **#15202B** fastest for reading among the shades tested. — [hrcak.srce.hr study (PDF)](https://hrcak.srce.hr/file/493249)
- NN/g (Budiu, 2020): for normal-vision users, **light mode (positive polarity) gives better reading performance**, and the advantage grows as font size shrinks. Dark mode may help some low-vision users. NN/g itself did not run original research and reviewed the literature. — [Dark mode vs light mode summary (Slashdot → nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/)](https://hardware.slashdot.org/story/20/02/16/2041240/dark-mode-vs-light-mode-which-is-better); [Fast Company interview](https://www.fastcompany.com/90421232); [Pixel Envy on NN/g](https://pxlnv.com/linklog/dark-mode-usability/)
- **The "AI purple" problem:** LLM-generated UIs default to purple because it is statistically common in training data and in AI/tech-startup sites. The recognizable "fingerprint" is **Inter + indigo→purple gradient + three rounded cards + a hero**, with the gradient used "in heroes, CTAs and backgrounds with no semantic meaning". One founder killed their purple gradient because "it made us look like every other AI tool in the category". The suggested fix: "use a gradient to draw the eye to one place, not as wallpaper." — [prg.sh: Why your AI keeps building the same purple gradient website](https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website); [925 Studios: AI slop design tells](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells); [Indie Hackers: The AI purple problem](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/the-ai-purple-problem-why-every-ai-brand-looks-the-same-6cb0aa2a02); [DEV: Fix the AI-generated look](https://dev.to/alanwest/how-to-fix-the-ai-generated-look-in-your-frontend-1ahh); [925 Studios: AI slop guide 2026](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-web-design-guide)
- Lando Norris SOTY: its neon accent is **taken from his helmet**, a brand color with a real-world origin. — [DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/best-designs/websites/lando-norris-website-design)
- WCAG 2.x contrast minimum (SC 1.4.3): **4.5:1** for normal text, **3:1** for large text (≥24px regular or ≥18.66px bold). Non-text UI components need **3:1** (SC 1.4.11). — [W3C Understanding SC 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html) *(standard known from prior knowledge; page not fetched this session)*

### Inferences (applied to LEVIK #14E0E0 → #A23BFF)
- **Base:** use a tinted near-black such as #0A0A0F–#0E0E14 (a slightly cool/violet tint ties it to the brand) rather than #000. Use 2–3 elevation steps (e.g., +4–8% lightness) for cards and nav.
- **Contrast:** teal #14E0E0 is very light. On a near-black it should clear 4.5:1 comfortably, so it works for text links and small UI. Purple #A23BFF is darker; on near-black it likely lands around the 3–4.5:1 border. Use it for **large display text, glows and graphics only**, not small body text. Verify both exact pairs with a contrast checker.
- **Differentiation from "AI purple":**
  1. Lead with **teal**, which is far less clichéd than indigo/violet, and let the purple arrive as the end of the gradient.
  2. Give the gradient **one job**, e.g., the "light leak / lens flare" motif, the active state, the play-reel cursor, or a thin underline. Do not use it as hero background wallpaper.
  3. Ground it in cinema: film grain, light leaks, anamorphic flares and color-grade language make the gradient read as *lighting*, not as SaaS decoration.
  4. Let the **video footage supply most of the color**. The UI stays near-monochrome (off-white, greys), and the gradient becomes an accent of about 5% of the surface.
  5. Avoid Inter + three rounded cards + a centered gradient hero.
- Because NN/g finds dark mode worse for long reading, keep Hebrew body text short, large (17–20px) and off-white, with generous line-height. Dark suits a showreel-led studio site, but long copy suffers.

- The Material Design dark-theme spec was not read directly (the 15.8:1 figure one blog attributes to Material is unverified).

## 4. Whitespace, grid and "less is more": how many sections a studio homepage needs

### Takeaway
Award-level studio sites keep the homepage to **a handful of large, single-purpose sections** with lots of negative space and let the work (video/3D) carry the page. Dark minimalism only works when the visuals are genuinely strong.

### Cited Findings
- Linear's site is cited as the biggest influence on dark-mode web design. A critique warns that dark minimalism "falls apart without genuinely good product visuals to fill the space". — [Anthony Hobday: Critique of the Linear website](https://anthonyhobday.com/blog/20230119.html); [Rectangle: The Linear effect](https://rectangle.substack.com/p/the-linear-effect)
- Lando Norris SOTY uses a **modular layout** that clearly separates two content worlds (on-track and off-track). — [DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/best-designs/websites/lando-norris-website-design)
- Kinetic type should be a **single hero moment**, not used across the whole page, a restraint principle. — [Envato 2026 trends](https://elements.envato.com/learn/web-design-trends)
- Editorial, asymmetric layouts are replacing uniform card grids. — [Envato 2026 trends](https://elements.envato.com/learn/web-design-trends)
- A "generic website is forgettable, and forgettable is fatal". — [Indie Hackers: AI purple problem](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/the-ai-purple-problem-why-every-ai-brand-looks-the-same-6cb0aa2a02)

### Inferences (recommended structure; not a sourced number)
- **Recommended LEVIK homepage: 6–7 sections** (more pushes into "cluttered"):
  1. **Hero**: full-bleed autoplay muted showreel loop, one Hebrew headline (kinetic once), one CTA ("לצפייה בשואוריל" / "בואו נדבר").
  2. **Selected work**: 3–6 projects as large video tiles (an asymmetric editorial grid, not a uniform bento of 12).
  3. **What we do**: 3–4 services in short lines (AI films, commercials, brand video, music video).
  4. **How it works / the AI edge**: one short process strip (brief → script → generation → edit/grade), which is where "it still sells" happens.
  5. **Social proof**: client logos plus 1–2 quotes or numbers.
  6. **CTA/contact**: one big closing line plus WhatsApp/email.
  7. (Optional) a short **about/founder** strip.
- One idea per viewport: section spacing of roughly 120–200px on desktop (about 80px on mobile), a 12-column grid with a wide gutter, and text widths of about 45–65 characters per line.
- Restraint budget: one display font, one text font; one accent gradient; one signature interaction (e.g., cursor-reactive video or grain); everything else static.

### Gaps
- No primary source gave a measured "ideal number of sections" for agency or studio homepages, so the 6–7 recommendation is inference.
- No conversion data comparing minimal and dense studio homepages was found.

## 5. RTL-specific design considerations (Hebrew)

### Takeaway
Mirror the **layout and the directional icons** (navigation, back/forward, progress), but **not** media-playback controls, clocks, handedness icons or numbers. Numbers and Latin words stay LTR inside RTL text. Hebrew has no caps and a different vertical structure, so it should not get Latin-style tracking or caps-based hierarchy, and its line-height and size need separate tuning from the Latin.

### Cited Findings
- Back/forward navigation buttons are **reversed**, and icons showing forward movement are **mirrored**. Icons with **handedness** or **media playback** function are **not mirrored** (most RTL users are right-handed). Icons that contain text need care. — [Material Design (m1): Bidirectionality](https://m1.material.io/usability/bidirectionality.html); [Material Design (m2): Bidirectionality](https://m2.material.io/go/design-bidirectionality)
- Numbers keep their natural direction; numbers and time icons (clocks) are **not mirrored**. Phone numbers and postal codes need attention. — [Wikimedia Codex: Bidirectionality](https://doc.wikimedia.org/codex/latest/style-guide/bidirectionality.html); [Cloudscape: Bidirectionality](https://cloudscape.design/foundation/core-principles/bidirectionality/)
- Further RTL/bidi references: [Mozilla Firefox RTL guidelines](https://firefox-source-docs.mozilla.org/code-quality/coding-style/rtl_guidelines.html); [Workday: RTL and Bidi](https://design.workday.com/globalization/rtl-and-bidi); [Adobe Spectrum: Bi-directionality](https://spectrum.adobe.com/page/bi-directionality)
- Hebrew has no capital letters, hangs from the top line, and its vav-height sits well above its x-height. Mixed Hebrew/English lines have mismatched line boxes. — [Ariela Housman](https://ariela-housman-x1ul.squarespace.com/blog/2018/2/5/why-hebrew-and-english-on-the-same-page-always-looks-terrible); [Meir Sadan](https://medium.com/@meirsadan/an-introduction-to-hebrew-type-98933e2fcb17)
- Hebrew is drawn at about 90% of Latin cap height in well-matched bilingual fonts. — [Canonical](https://canonical.com/blog/intial-hebrew-trials?format=md)

### Inferences (practitioner rules)
- **CSS:** `<html lang="he" dir="rtl">`; use logical properties (`margin-inline-start`, `padding-inline`, `inset-inline-start`) so the layout mirrors automatically. Wrap English brand terms or numbers in `<bdi>` or `dir="ltr"` spans when they sit at line edges, to prevent punctuation jumping (e.g., "LEVIK Production." ending up with the period on the wrong side).
- **Arrows:** a "next/continue" arrow points **left (←)** in Hebrew. A play ▶ stays pointing right (media is not mirrored). Carousels and sliders advance right-to-left. Horizontal scroll galleries should start from the right.
- **Video timelines and progress bars:** keep them LTR (media convention), per Material's "media playback not mirrored" rule.
- **Letter-spacing:** do not apply positive tracking or `text-transform: uppercase` styling to Hebrew (it has no caps). Use weight and size for hierarchy instead. Latin "eyebrow" labels (e.g., "AI FILM STUDIO") can keep their tracking; scope those styles with `:lang(en)`.
- **Line-height:** Hebrew body about 1.5–1.7, Hebrew display about 1.0–1.15. Set Latin separately with `:lang()` selectors or a `size-adjust`/`ascent-override` on the `@font-face` so mixed lines don't jump.
- **Numbers:** use Western (Arabic) numerals, which are standard in Israel. Use tabular figures for stats and counters, and keep phone numbers in LTR order.
- **Logo/wordmark:** a Latin "LEVIK" logo stays LTR; place it at the top-**right** (the RTL start position).
- **Reading pattern:** the eye enters top-right, so put the primary CTA and headline on the right or centered, and the secondary visual on the left.

### Gaps
- No authoritative Hebrew-specific source on CSS letter-spacing or line-height values was found. Those rules are practitioner inference.
- Material 3 (current) bidirectionality wording was not confirmed; m1 and m2 pages were used.
- No list of award-winning Hebrew RTL sites (Israeli SOTD winners) was obtained.
