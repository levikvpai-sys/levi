7 things every marketing video needs, before a single frame is shot.

By Levi Itshak Cohen, LEVIK Production · First place, Tel Aviv AI Film Festival 2026

These are the principles I check in every film before production starts. You can apply most of them today, on your own.

Quick wins
1

Lead with the most interesting moment.

Don't open with a logo or "Hi, I'm...". Open with the strongest moment in the video. The decision to keep watching is made in the first few seconds.

→ In a storyboard you choose the opening frame in advance.

2

One message per video.

If you need an "and also" to explain what the video is about, it's probably two videos.

→ A storyboard shows you right away if the story is drifting.

3

Make it work without sound.

Many people scroll with the sound off. Put the message on screen as text too, and make sure the picture tells the story on its own.

→ In a storyboard you plan the on-screen text for every shot.

4

Show, don't tell.

Instead of writing "personal service", show one moment of personal service. One image does what a whole paragraph can't.

→ A storyboard turns every description like that into a specific image.

5

End with one action.

"Message us on WhatsApp", not three different options. Tell the viewer what the next step is.

→ In a storyboard you plan the final frame and its line in advance.

The gap
6

Length and format are decided before the shoot.

A video for Instagram is built vertical; a video for a website or YouTube is built horizontal. Without planning, the picture gets cropped in the edit and the frame is ruined.

→ A storyboard marks the format of every shot.

7

Everything here happens on paper, before the shoot.

Opening, message, text, images, ending and format are six decisions. When they aren't made in advance, they get made in the edit, late and expensive. This is exactly where most videos get stuck.

→ A storyboard is where all these decisions are laid out, frame by frame.
